The log

2 October 2026 · Digital gold page

What was wrong: the storage row said storage is free for an initial period on most apps, "then a small annual fee". That came from a comparison by a competing platform, not from the providers' own terms, and their terms say more: SafeGold and Augmont take the charge every month, Augmont can sell part of your gold to cover it, and MMTC-PAMP can buy your gold back.

What it says now: storage is free for 2 years at SafeGold and 5 at MMTC-PAMP and Augmont; after that it is charged for, the charge can come out of your gold, and MMTC-PAMP can buy the gold back at its sell-back price. Sources: the terms of SafeGold, MMTC-PAMP and Augmont.

2 October 2026 · Compare page

What was wrong: the page said Taurus Ethical Fund and Quantum Ethical Fund are screened by ShariahCap Advisors. Neither fund's own documents name ShariahCap or any other Shariah board, so the claim had no reliable source.

What it says now: two of the six funds are certified by a Shariah board, both by TASIS; the other four name none in their own documents. It quotes what Taurus, Quantum and The Wealth Company say instead.

1 October 2026 · Digital gold page

What was wrong: the table said digital gold providers keep customers' gold in third-party vaults. MMTC-PAMP's own page describes the vaults as its own.

What it says now: the gold sits "in insured vaults — its own or a vault company's — with an independent trustee appointed to act for customers", resting on SafeGold's, MMTC-PAMP's and Augmont's own pages.

1 October 2026 · Compare page and home page

What was wrong: the compare page called all six funds Shariah-compliant and said all six are screened by a Shariah board. The Nippon ETF's own product note says it "is not a Shariah compliant scheme" and has no Shariah board, and The Wealth Company's board could not be verified. Both pages also said the Nifty500 Shariah index trails the Nifty 500 mainly because the screen removes financial services — a cause no source shows. And the compare page said its rows were in order of fund size, but the UTI index fund (₹88 crore) sat below two smaller funds.

What it says now: six funds "built around a Shariah screen", four of which name a board, with the Nippon position quoted from its product note. On the index, both pages now state only what NSE's factsheets show: the Shariah index is behind over one and five years, and financial services are about 31% of the Nifty 500 and almost none of the Shariah index. The rows are now in fund-size order, with sizes shown in the page's new returns table.

1 October 2026 · NPS page

What was wrong: several statements were out of date or inaccurate. The page said NPS has four asset classes, including A for alternative assets; that the equity class tracks broad market indices; that equity is capped at 75% for most subscribers and 50% for government employees and older subscribers; and that government staff joined NPS in place of a scheme they were already in.

What it says now: there are three asset classes, E, C and G — A was merged into C and E in December 2025. The equity class buys shares of large companies directly, mostly from the top 200 of the Nifty 250 index. Since October 2025, non-government subscribers can also pick pension fund schemes holding up to 100% equity, and central government staff can now choose 75% equity while young. The government row now says new government staff are enrolled in NPS. Sources: PFRDA's investment guidelines of 10 December 2025, its circulars of 28 August 2026 and 1 December 2025, and the Department of Financial Services. The exit table, which had two gaps, is now complete.

1 October 2026 · EPF page

What was wrong: the page said EPFO's investment pattern is notified by the Ministry of Labour and Employment. The Labour Ministry's own statement says the pattern is notified by the Department of Financial Services in the Ministry of Finance.

What it says now: "EPFO follows an investment pattern notified by the Department of Financial Services in the Ministry of Finance", resting on the Ministry of Labour and Employment's statement of 2 December 2024. The bands in the table did not change.

What gets logged

  • A figure, date or rate that was wrong
  • A source that does not say what the page said it says
  • A ruling quoted inaccurately, or credited to the wrong body or scholar
  • A claim that turns out to have no reliable source behind it

What doesn't: typos that don't change the meaning, and design changes. Routine updates, such as a new month's figure replacing last month's, change the page's "Last updated" date instead.

On the corrected page

The corrected page says so too. A line next to its "Last updated" date gives the date of the correction and links back to this log.
