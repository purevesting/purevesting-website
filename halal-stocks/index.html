How many Indian stocks are halal?

204 of the 501 companies in the Nifty 500 pass NSE's Shariah screen, as of 30 September 2026: about 41% of the companies, and about 31% of the Nifty 500's value. A company passes if its main business is halal and its debt, interest income, and cash and money owed to it stay under fixed limits, checked every month. Other screens set different limits, so a stock can pass one list and fail another.

Moin Ali Syed
Last updated 6 November 2026

The number, by companies and by value

Of the 501 companies in the Nifty 500 at the end of September 2026, 204 pass NSE's Shariah screen. Counted by market value, those that pass are a smaller share of the index, because the companies the screen removes are, on average, the bigger ones.

Share of the Nifty 500 that passes NSE's Shariah screen, by number of companies and by market value
Counted by Share that passes
Number of companies 204 of 501 40.7%
Market value Each company weighted by the value of its freely traded shares 31.4%

Banks, insurers and lenders (financial services) are the Nifty 500's biggest sector, and the screen removes them for what they do: they are 30.58% of the Nifty 500 and 0.18% of the Shariah index. The home page draws all 501 companies as squares, and the methodology page shows the sectors that remain.

What gets a company through

NSE's screen asks two questions, in order. First, the business: a company that is "primarily into" conventional finance, alcohol, pork, tobacco, gambling, narcotic drugs, or the kinds of entertainment and hospitality its rules name is out, whatever its numbers. Second, the money: a company that passes the first test must also stay under these limits.

  • Interest-based debt: up to 25% of total assets
  • Interest income, plus returns from interest-based investments: up to 2.5% of total income
  • Money owed to the company plus cash and bank balances: up to 90% of total assets

Some other screens, including the international Shariah standard, measure debt against the company's market value instead of its assets, and one big index company averages that value over 24 months. The methodology page puts them side by side, with an example you can change.

Good to know

It is one screen's list

The 204 are the companies NSE's screen lets through. Apps and other index makers use their own limits, so their lists differ — usually because the methods differ, not because anyone made a mistake. Why halal stock lists disagree.

It changes every month

NSE reviews its Shariah indices every month and removes any company its Shariah board finds no longer compliant. A company on the list today passed at the last review — not for all time.

Some income still needs purifying

The limits allow a small share of interest income; they don't make it clean. NSE's rules expect investors to give away their share of it (purification). The purification calculator works it out three ways.

Where to see the companies

To check one company, use the screener: all 501 Nifty 500 companies, each marked halal or not halal from NSE's index. NSE publishes the Nifty500 Shariah index's full list of companies on niftyindices.com, and its monthly factsheet shows the count, the sector weights and the ten largest holdings.

Of India's six Shariah mutual funds, one tracks this index: the UTI Nifty500 Shariah Index Fund. The compare page has all six side by side.
