How many Indian stocks are halal?

204 of the 501 companies in the Nifty 500 pass NSE's Shariah screen, as of 30 September 2026: about 41% of the companies, and about 31% of the Nifty 500's value.

Why less of the value passes

The companies the screen removes are, on average, the bigger ones. Banks, insurers and lenders (financial services) are the Nifty 500's biggest sector, and the screen removes them for what they do.

Financial services in the Nifty 500 30.58%
Financial services in the Shariah index 0.18%

The methodology page shows the sectors that remain.

What gets a company through

NSE's screen asks two questions, in order.

1

The business

A company that is "primarily into" conventional finance, alcohol, pork, tobacco, gambling, narcotic drugs, or the kinds of entertainment and hospitality its rules name is out, whatever its numbers.

2

The money

A company that passes the first test must also stay under these limits, checked every month.

Interest-based debt of total assets up to 25%
Interest income, plus returns from interest-based investments of total income up to 2.5%
Money owed to the company plus cash and bank balances of total assets up to 90%
Read more: other screens

Some other screens, including the international Shariah standard, measure debt against the company's market value instead of its assets, and one big index company averages that value over 24 months. The methodology page puts them side by side, with an example you can change.

Good to know

It is one screen's list

The 204 are the companies NSE's screen lets through. Apps and other index makers set different limits, so a stock can pass one list and fail another — usually because the methods differ, not because anyone made a mistake. Why halal stock lists disagree.

It changes every month

NSE reviews its Shariah indices every month and removes any company its Shariah board finds no longer compliant. A company on the list today passed at the last review — not for all time.

Some income still needs purifying

The limits allow a small share of interest income; they don't make it clean. NSE's rules expect investors to give away their share of it (purification). The purification calculator works it out three ways.

Where to see the companies
