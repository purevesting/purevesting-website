How much zakat do I owe?

Work out zakat for one person at a time. Count only what is yours.

  1. Add up what is yours: cash and bank money, gold and silver, savings, shares and business stock.
  2. Take off what you must pay now.
  3. If what is left reaches the nisab (₹94,487 by silver, as of 27 November 2026), zakat is 2.5% of all of it.

    Questions

    What is nisab?

    The minimum for zakat: the value of 612.36 g of silver, or of 87.48 g of gold. Zakat is due once what is yours, after what you must pay now, reaches it. Today's figure, with its date, is on the page Nisab today.

    Which gold and silver rate do you use?

    IBJA's latest closing rates (the India Bullion and Jewellers Association), with its own rates for 22K, 18K and 14K gold. You can type your jeweller's rate over them. A fatwa says today's market rate is better. The lower price a jeweller pays for old gold is also enough.

    Why do I see two numbers?

    Where scholars differ and you answer "Not sure", the calculator works out your zakat each way and shows the lowest and the highest, side by side. It never shows an average. Pick an answer to see one number, or ask a scholar you trust.

    Are my numbers saved anywhere?

    Only on this phone, and only when you tap Save. Nothing you type is sent to Purevesting or to anyone else. "Clear my numbers" deletes them.

    When do I pay?

    On your zakat date: the same Islamic date each year, one lunar year after you first had the minimum. If you use an English-calendar date, scholars say pay about 2.577%. Not sure of your date? Ask a scholar how to set one.

    What is never counted?

    Is there zakat on my house, car or furniture? No, not on things you use. But money you are saving for a house, a wedding or Hajj is counted.

    Also left out: clothes and the tools you earn with; term, health and vehicle insurance; gratuity and pension until they are paid; job shares that have not vested; a rent deposit you paid; a jeweller's gold scheme with no cash refund; diamonds and stones you wear; inheritance, salary and refunds not yet received; a child's own money; and interest or other haram money, which is given away instead.

    What it does

    It works out zakat for one person at a time, on that person's zakat date. Where scholars differ, it asks you. If you are not sure, it shows the lowest and the highest answer side by side, never an average, with the words "Ask a scholar you trust". It does not collect zakat, and your numbers stay on your phone.

    This calculator covers money, gold, silver, shares, savings and trade. Farm produce, animals and khums follow different rules. Ask a scholar you trust.

    The six steps

    1. Your zakat date: one lunar year after you first owned the nisab. Ups and downs during the year are left aside, unless your wealth falls to zero. On the date, everything counts, even money saved last month. With an English-calendar date, scholars say about 2.577%.
    2. Add up everything that is counted, at today's value.
    3. Take off what you owe.
    4. Compare what is left with the nisab.
    5. Zakat is 2.5% (one-fortieth) of the whole amount left, if it reaches the nisab. If not, nothing.
    6. Round to the nearest rupee.

    What is counted

    • Cash, bank accounts, wallets, UPI and money in other currencies (in rupees): in full. Bank interest is left out, to be given away.
    • Money lent and expected back: in full, every year. Committee (BC) money paid in and not yet received: in full.
    • Fixed and recurring deposits, PPF, NSC, KVP, senior-citizen and monthly-income schemes, bonds and debt funds: the money put in, not the interest.
    • PF: the compulsory part is left out until you receive it, then counted once a year has passed (question 5). Extra put in by choice (VPF): every year.
    • NPS: your own voluntary payments, every year. The compulsory and employer part: like compulsory PF (question 5).
    • Gold (coins, bars, men's gold, jewellery kept as savings): its weight × today's rate for its karat. 14K to 24K count by their whole weight; 9K and below is not counted as gold. Stones are left out: only the metal is weighed.
    • Women's jewellery worn regularly (question 2). Gold kept with a lender for a gold loan (question 7). Gold lent to someone, digital gold and EGR: as gold.
    • Sovereign Gold Bonds: their grams × today's 24K rate, without the interest. Gold ETFs and gold funds: today's value.
    • Silver (jewellery, coins, utensils): its weight × the silver rate × its purity.
    • Shares and funds bought to sell soon: their full market value. Kept for the long term, and vested job shares: question 4.
    • Crypto: today's value.
    • LIC endowment, money-back and whole-life policies: the premiums paid so far, minus money already paid back. A ULIP: the lower of its fund value and the premiums paid (question 6).
    • Business: cash, stock and money customers owe, in full. Stock at today's selling price. Customers' debts with no hope of payment are left out.
    • A plot or land: its market value, only if it was bought to sell (question 8). A flat or shop booked with a builder to resell: question 9.

    Never counted

    Your home, vehicle, furniture, clothes, and the tools or machines you earn a living with · term, health and vehicle insurance · gratuity and pension until they are paid · job shares that have not vested · a rent deposit you paid · a jeweller's gold scheme with no cash refund · diamonds and stones that are worn · inheritance, salary and refunds not yet received · a child's own money · haram money, which is given away instead.

    How things are valued

    • Gold and silver: IBJA's daily rates (24K 999 and silver 999), and IBJA's own 22K, 18K and 14K rates. You can type your own jeweller's rate. A fatwa says today's market rate is better, and a jeweller's buy-back rate is enough.
    • Weight in grams. 1 tola = 11.664 g; some jewellers call 10 g a tola.
    • Business stock, plots, shares, funds and crypto: what they would sell for today.

    What is taken off

    • Debts due now: bills, a credit-card bill, what a business owes its suppliers, prompt mehr that is unpaid.
    • Loans from family or friends with no fixed dates: in full.
    • Long loans paid in EMIs (home, car, education, personal, gold loan): question 3. It starts with the loan part of the next 12 months. The interest is never taken off.
    • Committee money received and still to pay: like a long loan. Builder instalments: the part due in the next 12 months. Deposits a landlord must give back.
    • Not taken off: mehr that is not yet due.

    The nisab

    • The weights: silver 612.36 g, gold 87.48 g.
    • Wealth of more than one kind: everything is added by value and compared with the silver nisab, unless you choose gold (question 1).
    • Only gold: the gold's weight, after debts, against 87.48 g. Only silver: the silver's weight, after debts, against 612.36 g.
    • With gold chosen: if the total is below the gold nisab, zakat is still due on any metal that reaches its own weight (silver 612.36 g, gold 87.48 g). Your debts come off your other wealth first.

    The questions where scholars differ

    Each question starts with the answer marked "start"; change it to what you follow.

    1. Which minimum (nisab) for money? Silver (start) · Gold.
    2. Women's jewellery worn regularly: Count it · Don't count it · Not sure (start).
    3. Long loans paid in EMIs: The loan part of the next 12 months (start) · The whole loan left · Not sure.
    4. Shares, funds and vested job shares kept for the long term: The full value (start) · A part of the value, the % your scholar gave you.
    5. Compulsory PF and NPS (and the employer's part): Leave it out until I receive it (start) · Count it now.
    6. LIC and ULIP premiums: Count them every year (start) · Not until money comes back · Not sure.
    7. Gold kept with a lender for a gold loan: Count it (start) · Don't count it while the lender has it · Not sure.
    8. Why did you buy the plot? Counted only "To sell".
    9. A flat or shop booked to resell: Count what I paid · Not until I get it (start) · Not sure.

    "Not sure" works out the lowest and the highest zakat over every open answer and shows both.
