Read more: how it is worked out

A SIP is treated as one payment at the start of every month, growing at the yearly return divided by twelve each month — the method most Indian SIP calculators use. A lump sum grows at the yearly return once a year.

Costs such as the fund's expense ratio, taken from your money every year, and any tax on gains are not taken off, so the real result is smaller. Real returns go up and down from year to year, and some years are negative.

A SIP is a way of buying, not a product: a standing instruction to buy units of a fund every month. The Shariah question is about the fund it buys — India's six Shariah funds are compared side by side. Shariah-screened funds can still earn a little interest, which many investors give away: the purification calculator works it out.
