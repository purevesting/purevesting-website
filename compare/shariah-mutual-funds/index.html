Halal mutual funds in India, compared

There are six Shariah-compliant mutual funds in India. They are not the same product in six wrappers: two are index trackers, four pick stocks, and they do not all answer to the same Shariah authority. This page puts them side by side and stops there.

All six funds are screened by a Shariah board. The column above records which one. They are not ranked, and a fund with a different board is not a lesser fund — Islamic finance has more than one recognised screening authority, and they do not always draw the line in the same place.

Of the six, TASIS — Taqwaa Advisory and Shariah Investment Solutions — certifies two: Tata Ethical Fund and the UTI Nifty500 Shariah Index Fund. TASIS states that Taurus Ethical Fund is not certified by TASIS. That is a statement about which board certifies which fund, and nothing more. Taurus and Quantum are screened by ShariahCap Advisors. Which authority you follow is a question for your scholar. Source: TASIS — as of 17 September 2026.

On Nippon India ETF Nifty 50 Shariah BeES: the scheme's own investment objective states that the scheme is not Shariah compliant. That is the fund house's own wording, not a judgement made here. Source: scheme information document.

How to read this table

The rows are not competitors

Two of these six track an index and four pick stocks. Comparing a stock-picking fund's return against an index tracker's return is comparing two different jobs. An index fund is meant to match its index, not beat it.

A different Shariah board is not a lower grade

The "Shariah screening by" column names the authority, it does not score it. Every fund in the table is screened. TASIS, ShariahCap Advisors and the other recognised boards apply their own reading of the same principles, and they sometimes differ on the edges. Picking between them is a question for a scholar, not for a table.

The screen has a cost, and it shows up in the index

Over both one year and five years, the Nifty500 Shariah index sits behind the Nifty 500. The main reason is structural: the screen removes roughly 31% of the market that sits in financial services. A fund tracking a Shariah index inherits that.

Fund size is not quality

The largest fund here is roughly ten times the size of the next one. Size reflects how long a fund has existed and how well it is distributed, not how it has performed.
