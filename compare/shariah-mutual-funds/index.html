Halal mutual funds in India, compared

There are six Shariah-compliant mutual funds in India. They are not the same product in six wrappers: two are index trackers, four pick stocks, and they do not all answer to the same Shariah authority. This page puts them side by side and stops there.

How to read this table

The rows are not competitors

Two of these six track an index and four pick stocks. Comparing a stock-picking fund's return against an index tracker's return is comparing two different jobs. An index fund is meant to match its index, not beat it.

"Certified" and "not certified" are not pass and fail

They record one specific fact: whether TASIS lists the fund as one it certifies. Taurus and Quantum answer to ShariahCap Advisors instead. Which authority to follow is a question for a scholar, not for a table on a website.

The screen has a cost, and it shows up in the index

Over both one year and five years, the Nifty500 Shariah index sits behind the Nifty 500. The main reason is structural: the screen removes roughly 31% of the market that sits in financial services. A fund tracking a Shariah index inherits that.

Fund size is not quality

The largest fund here is roughly ten times the size of the next one. Size reflects how long a fund has existed and how well it is distributed, not how it has performed.
