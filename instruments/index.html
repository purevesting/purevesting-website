Indian financial instruments, checked one by one

Indian financial instruments and where the Shariah rulings on them land
Instrument Where the rulings land Who decides you hold it
EPF Scholars differ. A fatwa from India treats the credit on the compulsory part as a bonus for service, not riba; others say it is riba and should be given away. On the voluntary top-up they agree much more closely. The law, for covered employees
PPF A fatwa says the interest is riba and may not be used; another says the account may still be used to save income tax if that interest is given away. You do — nobody is enrolled automatically
NPS Three questions in one product. A fatwa says the contributions may be taken, and profit known to come from haram investment given away. No fatwa was found either way on the annuity you must buy at exit. Depends how you joined — it replaced the old pension scheme for many government employees
Digital gold The question is possession, not interest. The Shariah standard on gold needs physical delivery or a specific, numbered bar; a 2026 analysis found Indian apps raise concerns on exactly that point. You do
Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana About 200 Indian scholars, in a 2019 resolution, and a Shariah board both say it is against Shariah because it pays interest. Money already in an account keeps earning until maturity. A parent or guardian, in the girl's name

The question that runs through all of them

One pattern stands out across these pages: the rulings often turn less on what a scheme pays than on whether you chose to be in it.

EPF and PPF show it best. Both pay a rate the government sets, and both lend most of the money on to governments at interest. Yet the same fatwa office gives opposite answers about them, and its reason has nothing to do with the return. With a compulsory provident fund, the money is cut before your salary reaches you, so it was never in your hands and you never agreed to anything. With PPF, you hold the money first and choose to deposit it.

Keep this in mind for anything you look at, even what isn't covered here yet. It is also why "Is it halal?" can have different answers for two people holding what looks like the same product.
