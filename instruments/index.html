Indian financial instruments, checked one by one

EPF

  • Compulsory: scholars differ
  • VPF: most say riba

Some fatwas say the credit on the compulsory part is not riba; on the voluntary top-up, most say it is.

PPF

  • Interest: riba
  • Account: may keep it

The interest is riba, but a fatwa says you may keep the account to save tax if you give the interest away.

NPS

  • Contributions
  • Haram profit
  • Annuity

You may take the contributions; give away profit from haram investments. No fatwa was found on the annuity.

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana

  • Against Shariah

Scholars in India say so, because it pays interest. Money already in an account keeps earning until maturity.

Digital gold

  • Possession: concerns

The question is possession, not interest, and a 2026 analysis found Indian apps raise concerns on exactly that.

What runs through all of them

The rulings often turn less on what a scheme pays than on whether you chose to be in it.

EPF and PPF show it best. Both pay a rate the government sets, yet the same fatwa office gives opposite answers about them. With a compulsory provident fund, the money is cut before your salary reaches you, so you never agreed to anything. With PPF, you hold the money first and choose to deposit it. It is also why "Is it halal?" can have different answers for two people holding what looks like the same product.
