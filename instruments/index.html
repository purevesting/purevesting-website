EPF Statute, for covered employees Bodies genuinely differ. Deoband treats the credit on the compulsory part as a bonus tied to service, not riba. Others hold it is riba and should be given away. On the voluntary top-up they converge much more tightly.

The question that runs through all of them

Read two of these pages and a pattern shows up that is easy to miss when you look at one instrument alone. The rulings very often turn less on what the scheme pays than on whether you chose to be in it.

EPF and PPF make the clearest pair. Both pay a rate the government sets. Both lend most of the money onward to governments at interest. Yet the same darul ifta reaches opposite conclusions about them, and the reason it gives has nothing to do with the return: with a compulsory provident fund the deduction happens before the wages ever reach you, so you never possessed the money and never contracted for anything. With PPF you hold the money first and choose to deposit it.

That distinction is worth carrying into any instrument you look at, including ones this site has not covered yet. It is also why "is it halal" often has a different answer for two people holding what looks like the same product.
