Is NPS halal?

NPS stacks three separate questions inside one product: two of its four asset classes are interest-bearing debt, its equity class tracks no Shariah screen, and above a corpus threshold it forces you to buy an annuity at the end. On the money going in, a fatwa addressing India's NPS directly held that your contribution and your employer's may both be taken, while profit traceable to forbidden investment should be given to the poor rather than used. On the forced annuity no ruling was found at all, and that is the part of NPS with the least clear answer.

Where your NPS money goes

Unlike EPF, you choose your own allocation in NPS. That matters here, because it means the composition of your account is partly your decision rather than something done to you.

The four asset classes

The four NPS asset classes and what each invests in
Class What it holds Pays a contracted return?
E — Equity Shares, through funds tracking broad market indices No — it is ownership, not a loan
C — Corporate bonds Debt issued by companies and public sector undertakings Yes
G — Government securities Loans to the central and state governments Yes
A — Alternative assets A small residual class, largely debt-backed instruments Mostly yes

Source: PFRDA — as of 18 September 2026.

The two problems this creates

First, C and G are interest-bearing debt. They are loans on which a contracted return is paid, which is the structure the riba prohibition addresses directly. Under Auto Choice these grow as a share of your account as you age, by design.

Second, the E class is not Shariah-screened. It tracks broad market indices, which include banks, non-banking financial companies, alcohol, tobacco and conventional insurance — the sectors a Shariah screen exists to remove. Equity itself is ownership rather than a loan, so E is not objectionable in structure; it is objectionable in contents, which is a different problem with a different remedy.

What you can and cannot control

What a subscriber can choose about their NPS allocation
Choice What it does
Active Choice You set the split across E, C, G and A yourself
Auto Choice The split is set by your age and shifts toward debt as you get older
Maximum equity 75% for most subscribers, 50% for government employees and older subscribers
Minimum debt You cannot reduce C, G and A to zero — some debt allocation is unavoidable

The last row is the one that matters most here: there is no allocation setting that removes interest-bearing debt from an NPS account entirely. Source: PFRDA scheme preference rules — as of 18 September 2026.

The annuity at the end

This is the part of NPS with no equivalent in EPF or PPF, and it is the part this page can least confidently answer.

When you exit at 60, you cannot simply take the money. Above a corpus threshold, a portion must be used to buy an annuity — a contract with a life insurance company that pays you a monthly income until death. You choose the insurer and the annuity variant, but not whether to buy one.

NPS exit rules and the minimum annuity requirement
Corpus at exit Minimum that must buy an annuity
Up to ₹8 lakh None — the whole corpus may be taken as a lump sum
Above ₹12 lakh At least 20%, with up to 80% taken as a lump sum

Two gaps in this table, stated rather than hidden. The band between ₹8 lakh and ₹12 lakh is deliberately left out: two sources describe it differently, and this site does not publish a figure it cannot pin to one source. Rules for government subscribers and for premature exit also differ and are not shown. These rules were changed by a PFRDA notification reported as December 2025, so anything you read elsewhere may predate the change. Source: PFRDA exit and withdrawal regulations — as of 18 September 2026.

Why this is a separate question

An annuity is not an investment held in your name. It is an insurance contract: you hand over a capital sum and receive payments whose total depends on how long you live. Conventional insurance has its own long literature in Islamic jurisprudence, distinct from the riba question, turning on uncertainty in the contract and on how the insurer invests the pool.

No ruling addressing the NPS annuity requirement specifically was found while researching this page. That is a statement about what was found, not a claim that none exists and not a verdict of any kind. If you know of one, send it to hello@purevesting.com and this page will be updated. Until then, this is the part of NPS most worth putting to a scholar directly, because it is the part that is forced on you at the end rather than chosen at the start.

What the standards say

IslamWeb — contributions may be taken, forbidden profit may not

A government employee in Karnataka, for whom NPS was compulsory, asked whether he could take the government's matching contribution as well as his own, given that the money is invested in the stock market. The answer separated the corpus into parts rather than ruling on NPS as a whole.

Both his own contribution and the government's were held to be unobjectionable. On the investment profit, the ruling turned on traceability, with the explicit qualifier that not all stock market investment is forbidden. Where profit is known to come from forbidden investment, it may not be benefited from — but the ruling also says not to leave it with the institution: take it and spend it on the poor and needy.

What the fatwa says "There is no harm on you to take what is deducted from your salary as well as what the government has added to what is deducted from you. As for the excess profits, if you know that it is the result of investing money in what is forbidden – and not all stock market investment is forbidden – then you are not permitted to benefit from it or to leave it (to them); rather, you should take it and spend it on the poor and needy. If you yourself are poor and needy, then it is permissible for you to take from it according to your need only." — Fatwa 352687, IslamWeb, dated 9 September 2017. Read the fatwa

What this ruling does and does not settle

What the IslamWeb fatwa on NPS covers and what it leaves open
Question Addressed?
Taking your own contribution Yes — no harm in taking it
Taking the employer or government contribution Yes — no harm in taking it
Profit traceable to forbidden investment Yes — not to be benefited from, and to be given to the poor
Voluntarily opening an NPS account Not addressed — the questioner's scheme was compulsory
The forced annuity purchase at exit Not addressed

The right-hand column records what this one fatwa covers. A blank is not a permission and not a prohibition — it means that question was not put to them.

Where NPS sits on the compulsory line

The EPF and PPF pages both turn on the same distinction: whether the money reached you before it went in, and whether you chose to put it there. NPS does not sit cleanly on either side of that line, and which side you are on depends on how you joined.

How different NPS subscribers joined, and what that means for the compulsory-voluntary distinction
How you joined Did you choose? Closest comparison
Central or state government employee who joined after the old pension scheme closed No — it replaced a scheme you were already in Nearest to compulsory EPF
Corporate NPS through an employer, deducted from salary Partly — the employer set it up, you opted in Between the two
You opened it yourself for the tax deduction Yes, entirely Nearest to PPF
Tier II account Yes — it is an optional savings account on top Nearest to PPF, with no lock-in

The rulings on the EPF and PPF pages turn on this distinction, so which row you are in is likely to matter. This table maps the rows; it does not extend any ruling to them. No body quoted on this site has been asked about the middle two rows.

What people do in practice

What follows describes approaches people take and the reasoning they give for them. It is not a list of options being recommended, and which one applies to you is a question for your scholar, not for this page.

Tilt Active Choice toward equity

Moving to Active Choice and setting equity at the maximum reduces the interest-bearing share as far as the rules allow. It does not remove it, and it does nothing about the equity class not being Shariah-screened — it changes the size of one problem, not the existence of either.

Contribute only what is compulsory

Where NPS came with the job, some people contribute the statutory minimum and direct further savings elsewhere, treating the compulsory and voluntary portions as different questions in the way the EPF rulings do.

Purify on the debt share

Relying on the ruling above, the portion of growth traceable to interest-bearing holdings is calculated from the annual statement and given away without expecting reward. NPS statements show returns per asset class, which makes this more tractable than in a blended fund.

Settle the annuity question early

The annuity is decided at 60 but shaped by choices made decades before, including the corpus you end up with. People who expect it to be a problem tend to raise it with a scholar long before exit rather than at the point of no return.

Your NPS statement, available from the CRA portal, shows contributions and returns broken out by asset class. That breakdown is what the purification approach above works from.
