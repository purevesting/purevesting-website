How NPS works

  1. Your money

    You pay in, and often your employer too. Unlike EPF, you choose how your money is split — or Auto Choice splits it by your age, shifting toward debt as you get older.

  2. Where it goes

    Into three asset classes:

    • E — shares of large listed companies Not Shariah-screened
    • C — corporate bonds Pays interest
    • G — government securities Pays interest

  3. What you get

    At 60, a lump sum. Above ₹8 lakh, part of it may have to buy an annuity: a contract with a life insurance company that pays you a monthly income for life.

In the main Tier I account, no setting removes interest-paying debt completely, and no Shariah-screened NPS scheme was found.

Read more: the three classes, and what you can choose

E holds shares of large listed companies, with at least 90% of the money in the top 200 of the Nifty 250 index. It is ownership, not a loan. C holds debt issued by companies and public sector undertakings; G holds loans to the central and state governments. Both pay a fixed return (interest).

There used to be a fourth class, A for alternative assets. The pension regulator, PFRDA, merged it into C and E in December 2025, so older articles may still list it.

C and G are loans that pay interest — exactly what the ban on riba is about. Under Auto Choice, their share of your account grows as you get older. E is not Shariah-screened: its shares come from India's biggest companies, which include banks, finance companies, alcohol, tobacco and normal insurance — the businesses a Shariah screen removes. Owning shares is not a loan, so E's problem is not how it works but what it holds. That is a different problem, with a different fix.

What a subscriber can choose about their NPS allocation
Choice What it does
Active Choice You set the split across E, C and G yourself. Equity is capped at 75% in the main Tier I account; the optional Tier II account allows up to 100%
Auto Choice The split is set by your age and shifts toward debt as you get older
A pension fund's own scheme Open to non-government subscribers since October 2025. The fund sets the mix inside a band; the most aggressive band holds 80–100% equity
Government subscribers A separate menu. For central government staff, the highest-equity option holds 75% while you are young and falls with age

The closest to no interest is the most aggressive pension fund scheme, which may hold up to 20% debt, or none, as the fund decides — and only non-government subscribers can pick it. Tier II can be all shares, but it is a separate savings account, not the pension.

Read more: the annuity at the end

When you leave at 60, you can't just take all the money. Above a certain amount, part of it must buy an annuity. You choose the insurer and the type, but not whether to buy one. EPF and PPF have nothing like it.

Minimum share of the NPS money that must buy an annuity, leaving at 60
Your NPS money at 60 Minimum annuity
Up to ₹8 lakh None — the whole amount may be taken as a lump sum
₹8–12 lakh None if you take up to ₹6 lakh as a lump sum and the rest in instalments over at least 6 years. Otherwise at least 20% — 40% for government subscribers
Above ₹12 lakh At least 20%, with up to 80% as a lump sum. Government subscribers: at least 40%, with up to 60% as a lump sum

Leaving before 60 is stricter for everyone: up to ₹5 lakh can all be taken as a lump sum, and above that at least 80% must buy an annuity. These rules are from December 2025, so older articles may be out of date. The retirement income schemes added in May 2026 give new ways to draw the rest, but don't change these minimums.

An annuity is not an investment in your name. It is an insurance contract: you hand over a lump sum, and how much you get back depends on how long you live. Scholars have long treated normal insurance as its own question, apart from riba: it turns on the uncertainty in the contract, and on how the insurer invests the money.

No fatwa on the NPS annuity itself was found. If you know of one, send it to salam@purevesting.com and I'll add it. Until then, this is the part of NPS most worth asking a scholar about, because it is forced on you at the end, not chosen at the start.

What scholars say

A fatwa on India's NPS

  • Your own contribution: no harm in taking it
  • The employer's or government's: no harm in taking it
  • Profit you know comes from haram investment: not to be used, and to be given to the poor
  • Opening an NPS account by choice: not asked — his NPS was compulsory
  • The annuity you must buy at exit: not asked

A government employee in Karnataka, for whom NPS was compulsory, asked if he could take the government's contribution as well as his own, since the money is invested in the stock market. The fatwa split the money into parts, and says clearly that not all stock market investment is forbidden.

What the fatwa says "There is no harm on you to take what is deducted from your salary as well as what the government has added to what is deducted from you. As for the excess profits, if you know that it is the result of investing money in what is forbidden – and not all stock market investment is forbidden – then you are not permitted to benefit from it or to leave it (to them); rather, you should take it and spend it on the poor and needy. If you yourself are poor and needy, then it is permissible for you to take from it according to your need only." — Fatwa 352687, 9 September 2017.

Compulsory, or your choice?

On EPF and PPF, the answer turns on whether you chose to put the money in. NPS can be either, depending on how you joined.

What people do

Put more in shares

Active Choice at its 75% equity maximum — or, if you are not a government subscriber, the most aggressive pension fund scheme — makes the interest part smaller. Neither guarantees it is gone, or fixes the shares not being Shariah-screened.

Pay only what is compulsory

Where NPS came with the job, some pay only the compulsory minimum and save the rest elsewhere, as with the EPF fatwas.

Give away the interest part

Following the fatwa, they work out the growth that came from interest-paying holdings and give it away. NPS statements show returns for each asset class, which makes this easier.

Think about the annuity early

It is decided at 60, but shaped by choices made decades before. People who expect it to be a problem ask a scholar long before they leave.

Your NPS statement, from the CRA portal, shows contributions and returns for each asset class.
