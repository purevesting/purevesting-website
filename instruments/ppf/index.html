Is PPF halal?

PPF pays a rate the government fixes each quarter on money you chose to deposit, and every ruling found here treats that credit as riba. Darul Uloom Deoband says so directly, and draws the contrast with EPF itself: there the deduction happens before the wages reach you, here you own the money and chose to lend it. The same darul ifta has separately said the account may be used to save income tax provided the interest is given to the poor without seeking reward — so the question has one answer for the account and a different one for the interest.

How PPF earns, and where the money goes

PPF is simpler than EPF, and the simplicity is what makes the ruling on it less contested. There is no fund manager, no equity portion and no market risk. You deposit, the government credits a rate it sets, and it pays you back with the credit at the end.

How the PPF scheme works
How it works Detail
Who sets the rate The Ministry of Finance, announced every quarter
Rate 7.1% a year, compounded annually
Deposit limits ₹500 minimum and ₹1,50,000 maximum in a financial year
Lock-in 15 years, then extendable in 5-year blocks
Tax treatment Deduction under Section 80C, tax-free interest, tax-free maturity
Who opens the account You do. Nobody is enrolled automatically.

Source: Ministry of Finance quarterly notification — rate as notified for April–June 2026.

Where your deposit actually goes

PPF deposits are credited to the National Small Savings Fund. That fund invests the collections in special central and state government securities — which is to say it lends the money to governments at interest. The rate you receive and the rate the governments pay are set separately, but the underlying transaction is a loan on which a contracted return is paid.

Source: National Small Savings Fund — as of 18 September 2026.

What the standards say

Both rulings below are from the Darul Ifta at Darul Uloom Deoband, an Indian institution ruling on Indian schemes. They were issued years apart, to different questioners, and they answer two different questions. Read together they are not in conflict.

On the interest: it is riba, and it is not yours to use

Asked whether PPF interest is like the interest on a government provident fund, the darul ifta said no. Because PPF is voluntary and the depositor owns the money being deposited, the credit on it is interest in the Shariah sense and it is not lawful to use. The ruling directs that it be given to the poor without seeking reward for it.

What the fatwa says Since PPF is voluntary and the depositor owns the invested amount, "the interest which is levied on it shall be interest as per the government rules as well as according to Shariah. Hence it is not lawful to use it." The ruling adds that the interest should be given to the poor without seeking reward, and that zakah is due on the deposited amount but not on the interest. — Fatwa 894/876/SN=09/1437, Darul Ifta Deoband. Read the fatwa

On the account: it may be used to save income tax

Asked by someone who wanted the Section 80C tax deduction and proposed giving the interest away to the poor, the darul ifta answered that the account may be used for that purpose, on that condition. This is the ruling most people actually need, because saving tax is usually why a PPF account gets opened in the first place.

What the fatwa says "Yes, you can avail PF to save your hard-earned income from income tax. However, the extra amount which you receive on it, give it in charity to poor and needy ones without the intention of reward." — Fatwa 634/510/B=1431, Darul Ifta Deoband. Read the fatwa

On the absence of a competing ruling

On EPF, bodies genuinely disagree, and that page sets four positions side by side. On PPF no ruling holding the opposite was found while researching this page. That is not the same as proving none exists, and it is not a claim of consensus — it is a statement about what was found. If you know of a darul ifta or scholar holding that PPF interest is not riba, send it to hello@purevesting.com and this page will be updated with it.

Why PPF and EPF get different answers

This is the part worth understanding, because the same institution reaches opposite conclusions about two schemes that both pay a government-set rate. The difference is not the rate and not the counterparty. It is possession and choice.

The difference between PPF and a compulsory provident fund under the Deoband reasoning
PPF Compulsory provident fund
Who decides you contribute You do Statute does
Did the money reach you first Yes — you deposit money you already hold No — it is deducted before the wages reach you
Did you contract for a return Yes, by opening the account No, you were never offered the choice
How the credit is characterised Interest, in the Shariah sense A bonus tied to service, not riba

This table sets out the reasoning in Fatwa 894/876/SN=09/1437, which contrasts PPF with a government provident fund directly. It adds nothing to the ruling. Other bodies reason differently about the compulsory case — see the EPF page for the positions that disagree.

The practical consequence is that "I have both, so the same answer applies to both" does not hold. A salaried person with an EPF account and a PPF account is holding two instruments that the same darul ifta treats differently, for a reason that has nothing to do with either one's return.

What people do in practice

What follows describes approaches people take and the reasoning they give for them. It is not a list of options being recommended, and which one applies to you is a question for your scholar, not for this page.

Keep the account, give the interest away

The approach the second fatwa above describes: use the account for the Section 80C deduction, track the interest credited each year from the passbook, and give that amount to the poor without expecting reward for it. The deposit itself is your own money throughout.

Use a different 80C deduction

Section 80C is a single limit that several things can fill, not a PPF-specific allowance. People who would rather not hold the instrument at all fill the limit from what they are already paying or holding elsewhere, and treat the tax question as separate from the investment question.

Stop new deposits, leave the balance

PPF has a 15-year lock-in and limited partial withdrawal from the seventh year, so an account cannot simply be closed on demand. Some people stop contributing while leaving the existing balance to run, and purify the interest credited in the meantime.

Ask before unwinding an account

Closing or extending a PPF account has tax and lock-in consequences that are not Shariah questions at all, and a child's education savings often sits inside one. Getting specific guidance is generally advised over acting abruptly.

Your PPF passbook shows interest credited separately from your deposits, which is the figure the purification approaches above work from. It is available from the bank or post office holding the account, and in most net-banking portals.
