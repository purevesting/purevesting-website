Is PPF halal?

Every fatwa found says PPF interest is riba (interest), because you chose to put your own money in. But a fatwa also says you may use a PPF account to save income tax, if you give the interest to the poor without expecting reward.

Moin Ali Syed
Last updated 6 November 2026
On this page
  1. How PPF works, and where the money goes
  2. What scholars say
  3. Why PPF and EPF get different answers
  4. What people do

How PPF works, and where the money goes

PPF is simpler than EPF, which is why scholars disagree less about it. There is no fund manager, no shares and no market risk. You deposit money, the government adds interest at a rate it sets, and it pays everything back at the end.

How the PPF scheme works
How it works Detail
Who sets the rate The Ministry of Finance, announced every quarter
Rate 7.1% a year, compounded annually
Deposit limits ₹500 minimum and ₹1,50,000 maximum in a financial year
Lock-in 15 years, then extendable in 5-year blocks
Tax treatment Deduction under Section 80C, tax-free interest, tax-free maturity
Who opens the account You do. Nobody is enrolled automatically.

Where your money goes

Your PPF money goes into the National Small Savings Fund. That fund buys special central and state government securities — in other words, it lends the money to governments at interest. The rate you get and the rate the governments pay are set separately, but underneath it is a loan that pays a fixed return.

What scholars say

Both fatwas below come from the same fatwa office in India and are about Indian schemes. They were given years apart, to different people, and answer two different questions. They don't contradict each other.

The interest: it is riba, and not yours to use

Asked if PPF interest is like the interest on a government provident fund, the fatwa said no. PPF is voluntary and the money is yours, so what it adds is interest in the Shariah sense, and you may not use it. It should be given to the poor, without expecting reward. The same fatwa says zakat is due on what you deposited, but not on the interest.

What the fatwa says Since PPF is voluntary and the depositor owns the invested amount, "the interest which is levied on it shall be interest as per the government rules as well as according to Shariah. Hence it is not lawful to use it." The ruling adds that the interest should be given to the poor without seeking reward, and that zakah is due on the deposited amount but not on the interest. — Fatwa 894/876/SN=09/1437.

The account: you may use it to save income tax

Someone asked if they could use a PPF account to save income tax, and give the interest to the poor. The fatwa said yes, on that condition. This is the answer most people need, because saving tax is usually why a PPF account is opened.

What the fatwa says "Yes, you can avail PF to save your hard-earned income from income tax. However, the extra amount which you receive on it, give it in charity to poor and needy ones without the intention of reward." — Fatwa 634/510/B=1431.

No fatwa found that says otherwise

On EPF, scholars really do disagree. On PPF, no fatwa saying the opposite was found. If you know of one that says PPF interest is not riba, send it to salam@purevesting.com and I'll add it here.

Why PPF and EPF get different answers

Both pay a rate the government sets, yet the same fatwa office gives opposite answers about them. The difference is not the rate, and not who the money is lent to. It is whether the money was yours first, and whether you chose.

The difference between PPF and a compulsory provident fund, in the fatwa's reasoning
PPF Compulsory provident fund
Who decides you pay in You do The law does
Did the money reach you first Yes — you deposit money you already hold No — it is cut before your salary reaches you
Did you agree to a return Yes, by opening the account No, you were never given the choice
What the extra amount is Interest, in the Shariah sense A bonus for your service, not riba

Some scholars see the compulsory case differently: the EPF page has their views.

So if you have both EPF and PPF, one answer doesn't cover both. The same fatwa office treats them differently, for a reason that has nothing to do with what either one pays.

What people do

Keep the account, give the interest away

What the second fatwa above describes: use the account for the Section 80C tax deduction, note the interest added each year in the passbook, and give that amount to the poor without expecting reward. Your deposit stays your own money.

Use a different 80C deduction

Section 80C is one limit that several things can fill, not just PPF. People who would rather not hold PPF at all fill the limit with something else they already pay or hold, and keep the tax question separate from the investment question.

Stop new deposits, leave the balance

PPF is locked for 15 years, with only limited withdrawals from the seventh year, so you can't just close it. Some people stop paying in, leave the balance where it is, and give away the interest added in the meantime.

Ask before closing an account

Closing or extending a PPF account has tax and lock-in effects that aren't Shariah questions at all, and it often holds a child's education savings. People usually ask first instead of acting in a hurry.

Your PPF passbook shows the interest separately from your deposits. You can get it from your bank or post office, and in most net banking.
