Is digital gold halal?

The question is possession, not interest.

The Shariah standard on gold

Gold on the spot

In your hand, or a specific, numbered bar set aside for you that same day.

Indian digital gold apps

Raise concerns

They don't show that, and a 2026 analysis found this raises Shariah concerns.

Last updated 8 November 2026

How digital gold works

  1. Your money

    You buy on PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm or a jeweller's app, from as little as ₹1, plus 3% GST that isn't refunded when you sell.

  2. Where it goes

    The app is not the seller. It passes your order to MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold or Augmont, which keep physical gold in insured vaults — their own or a vault company's — with an independent trustee appointed to act for customers.

  3. What you get

    A quantity in grams, recorded in your account — often a small fraction of a gram, not tied to any specific bar.

    Coins or bars only later, above a minimum (from 0.5 g at SafeGold), with a charge for minting and delivery.

Read more: storage, delivery and who regulates it

Storage is free for a set time — 2 years at SafeGold, 5 at MMTC-PAMP and Augmont. After that, storage is charged for, and the charge can come out of your gold. MMTC-PAMP can also buy your gold back, at its sell-back price, once the 5 years are up. Augmont's terms also say you must take delivery of your gold within 10 years of buying it.

The smallest purchase is ₹1 at SafeGold and ₹10 at Augmont. Digital gold is regulated by neither SEBI nor RBI.

In November 2025, SEBI warned that digital gold is outside its rules: it is not a security, nor a regulated commodity contract, and none of SEBI's investor protections apply to it. That is not a Shariah point, but it matters for the same reason possession does: if the company fails, what you actually own decides what you get back.

What scholars say

Gold pays no interest. The Shariah question about it is possession, because gold is one of the six things whose exchange the hadith on riba sets rules for.

The Shariah standard on gold, 2016

  • Same sitting: gold and money handed over together — physically, or by the gold becoming legally yours on the spot
  • Legally yours: a specific bar, told apart by its serial number, set aside for you — or a certificate for one, issued the same day
  • Gold set aside for no one (unallocated): not to be sold unless it is physically handed over

Issued in November 2016 by the body whose Shariah standards most Islamic banks and finance companies follow. These two conditions decide the digital gold question.

What the standard says Clause 3/2/1: "In case of sale of gold for gold, or silver or currencies, the two counter-values must be delivered during the contracting session, physically or constructively." Clause 3/4: "Constructive possession is realized by allocation of the ingot and by enabling the buyer to dispose of it, or by holding a certificate that represents ownership of a specified ingot that is distinguishable (an allocated ingot) from others, by serial numbers or other distinct marks from other ingots, provided the certificate is issued the day the contract is concluded … it is not permissible to sell an unspecified ingot (technically known in the market as unallocated ingot) without physical possession." Clause 3/5/4: "Ingots stored in a warehouse and not distinguishable by serial numbers are considered unallocated and are thus subject to the Shari'ah rulings for joint ownership." — Shari'ah Standard No. 57, Gold and Its Trading Controls, issued 19 November 2016.

A 2026 analysis

  • Digital gold: raises Shariah concerns about possession
  • Sovereign Gold Bonds: not Shariah-compliant, because of their fixed interest

Published in March 2026, it checked India's gold market against this standard: you buy small units that stay with the company, without separate ownership records or a clear right to delivery straight away. It is an analysis by one author, not a fatwa.

What the analysis says On digital gold: "investors purchase fractional gold units that remain under the platform's custody without a clear right to immediate delivery, contravening [the standard's] requirement for qabḍ (possession)." On Sovereign Gold Bonds: they "involve fixed interest payments and cash settlement, rendering them non-compliant with Sharīʿah principles." — Analysis published 26 March 2026, updated 6 April 2026.

No fatwa was found saying Indian digital gold, as sold today, meets the possession rule. If you know of one, send it to salam@purevesting.com and I'll add it.

The gold standard's conditions set against what public information shows about Indian digital gold
What the standard requires What public information on Indian digital gold shows
Gold and money exchanged in the same session (3/2/1) Gold is credited to your account when you pay. Whether bars are bought and set aside that same day is not stated publicly.
A specific bar, identified by serial number, allocated to you (3/4) Your holding is a quantity in grams, often a fraction of a gram. It is not shown tied to any specific bar.
Or a certificate for a specified bar, issued the same day (3/4) No such certificate is described in the public information reviewed.
Unallocated gold not sold without physical possession (3/4) Physical delivery exists, but only later, as minted coins, above a minimum, and with charges.

A provider that does set aside specific, numbered bars and gives a same-day certificate would be in a different position under this standard. That is what to ask before buying.

Four ways to hold gold

Each raises a different Shariah question, and one of them isn't really gold at all.

Physical gold

What you own
The metal itself, in your hands
Regulated by
Purity hallmarking by BIS
Pays interest?
No
The Shariah question
Spot exchange and possession at the counter

Digital gold

What you own
A recorded quantity in shared, trustee-held vaults
Regulated by
Neither SEBI nor RBI
Pays interest?
No
The Shariah question
Whether that recorded quantity counts as possession — see above

Gold ETF

What you own
Units of a fund that holds physical gold
Regulated by
SEBI
Pays interest?
No
The Shariah question
The standard applies its gold rules to funds that hold only gold (10/3), so possession is again the question

Sovereign Gold Bond

What you own
A loan to the Government of India, repaid at the gold price
Regulated by
RBI, for the Government of India
Pays interest?
Yes — 2.5% a year on the amount invested
The Shariah question
Interest on a loan — a different question from possession entirely
Read more: Sovereign Gold Bonds

The government hasn't issued new Sovereign Gold Bonds since February 2024; in July 2025 it told the Rajya Sabha that new issues were paused for prudent debt management. Bonds already issued keep paying 2.5% a year, credited twice a year, and can be sold on the stock exchange if held in demat form.

They are often called the "safest" way to hold gold: backed by the government, no storage worry, no GST. But an SGB is not gold you own. It is money lent to the government, repaid at whatever gold is worth at the end, with interest paid along the way. The gold price only measures the loan; it is not what you hold.

What people do

Buy physical gold instead

Paying for coins or bars and taking them home in the same visit is the simplest way to meet the on-the-spot rule, even with the storage and purity worries it brings.

Take delivery of what they hold

Turning a digital gold balance into coins ends the shared-vault question for that gold. It costs minting and delivery charges, so it suits bigger balances.

Ask the provider two questions first

Is my gold tied to a specific, numbered bar? Do I get a certificate for it on the day I buy? The answers differ from company to company.

Treat existing SGBs separately

There the question is interest, not possession. Existing bonds pay into your bank account twice a year and can be sold on the stock exchange before they mature.
