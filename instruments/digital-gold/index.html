Is digital gold halal?

The question is not interest but possession. The Shariah standard on gold says you must get it on the spot: in your hand, or as a specific, numbered bar set aside for you that same day. Indian digital gold apps don't show that, and a 2026 analysis found this raises Shariah concerns.

Last updated 6 November 2026

How digital gold works

When you buy gold on PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm or a jeweller's app, the app is not the seller. It passes your order to one of a few companies — MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold and Augmont — which keep physical gold in insured vaults and record how many grams are yours.

How digital gold in India is bought and held
How it works Detail
Who holds the gold The provider, in insured vaults — its own or a vault company's — with an independent trustee appointed to act for customers
What you hold A quantity in grams, recorded in your account — often a small fraction of a gram
Smallest purchase As little as ₹1 (SafeGold) or ₹10 (Augmont)
Tax on purchase 3% GST, added to the price you pay and not refunded when you sell
Physical delivery Available as coins or bars above a minimum (from 0.5 g at SafeGold), with a charge that covers minting and delivery
Storage Free for a set time — 2 years at SafeGold, 5 at MMTC-PAMP and Augmont. After that, storage is charged for, and the charge can come out of your gold. MMTC-PAMP can also buy your gold back, at its sell-back price, once the 5 years are up
Regulated by Neither SEBI nor RBI

Augmont's terms also say you must take delivery of your gold within 10 years of buying it.

What SEBI has said about it

In November 2025, SEBI warned that digital gold is outside its rules: it is not a security, nor a regulated commodity contract, and none of SEBI's investor protections apply to it. That is not a Shariah point, but it matters for the same reason possession does: if the company fails, what you actually own decides what you get back.

What scholars say

Gold is different from EPF, PPF and NPS, where the question is interest. Gold pays no interest. The Shariah question about it is possession, not interest, because gold is one of the six things whose exchange the hadith on riba sets rules for.

The Shariah standard on gold: on the spot, and a specific bar

In November 2016, the body whose Shariah standards most Islamic banks and finance companies follow issued a standard just for gold. Two of its conditions decide the digital gold question.

First, when you buy gold with money, both sides must be handed over in the same sitting — physically, or by the gold becoming legally yours on the spot. No delivery later. Second, "legally yours" has an exact meaning: a specific bar, told apart from others by its serial number, must be set aside for you, or you must hold a certificate for such a bar issued the same day. Gold not set aside for anyone (unallocated gold) may not be sold unless it is physically handed over.

What the standard says Clause 3/2/1: "In case of sale of gold for gold, or silver or currencies, the two counter-values must be delivered during the contracting session, physically or constructively." Clause 3/4: "Constructive possession is realized by allocation of the ingot and by enabling the buyer to dispose of it, or by holding a certificate that represents ownership of a specified ingot that is distinguishable (an allocated ingot) from others, by serial numbers or other distinct marks from other ingots, provided the certificate is issued the day the contract is concluded … it is not permissible to sell an unspecified ingot (technically known in the market as unallocated ingot) without physical possession." Clause 3/5/4: "Ingots stored in a warehouse and not distinguishable by serial numbers are considered unallocated and are thus subject to the Shari'ah rulings for joint ownership." — Shari'ah Standard No. 57, Gold and Its Trading Controls, issued 19 November 2016.

A 2026 analysis: Indian apps raise concerns

An analysis published in March 2026 checked India's gold market against this standard. On digital gold, it found that you buy small units that stay with the company, without separate ownership records or a clear right to delivery straight away — and that this raises Shariah concerns about possession. It was firmer on Sovereign Gold Bonds: their fixed interest makes them not Shariah-compliant. It is an analysis by one author, not a fatwa.

What the analysis says On digital gold: "investors purchase fractional gold units that remain under the platform's custody without a clear right to immediate delivery, contravening [the standard's] requirement for qabḍ (possession)." On Sovereign Gold Bonds: they "involve fixed interest payments and cash settlement, rendering them non-compliant with Sharīʿah principles." — Analysis published 26 March 2026, updated 6 April 2026.

No fatwa found that says otherwise

No fatwa was found saying Indian digital gold, as sold today, meets the possession rule. If you know of one, send it to salam@purevesting.com and I'll add it.

The possession test, point by point

The gold standard's conditions set against what public information shows about Indian digital gold
What the standard requires What public information on Indian digital gold shows
Gold and money exchanged in the same session (3/2/1) Gold is credited to your account when you pay. Whether bars are bought and set aside that same day is not stated publicly.
A specific bar, identified by serial number, allocated to you (3/4) Your holding is a quantity in grams, often a fraction of a gram. It is not shown tied to any specific bar.
Or a certificate for a specified bar, issued the same day (3/4) No such certificate is described in the public information reviewed.
Unallocated gold not sold without physical possession (3/4) Physical delivery exists, but only later, as minted coins, above a minimum, and with charges.

A provider that does set aside specific, numbered bars and gives a same-day certificate would be in a different position under this standard. That is what to ask before buying.

Digital gold next to other ways to hold gold

The four common ways to hold gold raise different Shariah questions, and one of them isn't really gold at all.

Four ways to hold gold in India and the Shariah question each raises
Format What you actually own Regulated by Pays interest? The Shariah question
Physical gold The metal itself, in your hands Purity hallmarking by BIS No Spot exchange and possession at the counter
Digital gold A recorded quantity in shared, trustee-held vaults Neither SEBI nor RBI No Whether that recorded quantity counts as possession — see above
Gold ETF Units of a fund that holds physical gold SEBI No The standard applies its gold rules to funds that hold only gold (10/3), so possession is again the question
Sovereign Gold Bond A loan to the Government of India, repaid at the gold price RBI, for the Government of India Yes — 2.5% a year on the amount invested Interest on a loan — a different question from possession entirely

The government hasn't issued new Sovereign Gold Bonds since February 2024; in July 2025 it told the Rajya Sabha that new issues were paused for prudent debt management. Bonds already issued keep paying 2.5% a year, credited twice a year, and can be sold on the stock exchange if held in demat form.

Why the SGB row matters

Sovereign Gold Bonds are often called the "safest" way to hold gold: backed by the government, no storage worry, no GST. But an SGB is not gold you own. It is money lent to the government, repaid at whatever gold is worth at the end, with interest paid along the way. The gold price only measures the loan; it is not what you hold.

What people do

Buy physical gold instead

Paying for coins or bars and taking them home in the same visit is the simplest way to meet the on-the-spot rule. That is why some people who hold gold for Shariah reasons prefer it, even with the storage and purity worries it brings.

Take delivery of what they hold

Turning a digital gold balance into coins ends the shared-vault question for that gold. It costs minting and delivery charges, and the balance must reach the app's minimum, so it suits bigger balances more than small ones.

Ask the provider two questions first

Is my gold tied to a specific, numbered bar? Do I get a certificate for it on the day I buy? The standard's test turns on these two facts, and they differ from company to company — so the answer for one app may not hold for another.

Treat existing SGBs separately

There the question is interest, not possession, so it is a separate question from digital gold. Existing bonds pay into your bank account twice a year and can be sold on the stock exchange before they mature, which changes what your options are.
