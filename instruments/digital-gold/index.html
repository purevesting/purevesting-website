Is digital gold halal?

The question for digital gold is not interest but possession: AAOIFI's gold standard requires that gold bought with money be exchanged on the spot, with possession taken either physically or through a specific, serial-numbered bar allocated to you that same day. Indian digital gold is sold in fractions of a gram and held by a trustee in shared vaults, public information on the providers does not show any customer's gold tied to a specific bar, and a 2026 analysis applying the AAOIFI standard to Indian platforms concluded they raise Shariah concerns on exactly that point. Two warnings sit alongside it: SEBI has said digital gold is outside its regulation entirely, and Sovereign Gold Bonds — the gold product that looks safest — pay interest on top of the gold price.

How digital gold actually works

When you buy gold on PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm or a jeweller's app, the app is not the seller. It passes your order to one of a small number of companies — MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold and Augmont — which hold physical gold in insured vaults and record how many grams belong to you.

How digital gold in India is bought and held
How it works Detail
Who holds the gold The provider, in third-party vaults, with an independent trustee holding title on customers' behalf
What you hold A quantity in grams, recorded in your account — often a small fraction of a gram
Smallest purchase As little as ₹1 on some apps
Tax on purchase 3% GST, not recovered when you sell
Physical delivery Available as minted coins or bars above an app-set minimum, with minting and delivery charges
Storage Free for an initial period on most apps, then a small annual fee
Regulated by Neither SEBI nor RBI

What SEBI has said about it

In November 2025 SEBI warned investors that digital gold products operate outside its regulatory purview, are not recognised as securities or regulated commodity derivatives, and are not covered by any of the investor protections that apply in the securities market. That has nothing to do with Shariah, but it matters for the same reason the possession question does: if the provider fails, what you actually own decides what you get back.

Source: SEBI caution, as reported by All India Radio — dated 8 November 2025.

What the standards say

Gold is different from the instruments on the other pages of this site. EPF, PPF and NPS turn on interest. Gold carries no interest at all — the Shariah question about it is possession, not interest, because gold is one of the six commodities whose exchange the hadith on riba regulates directly.

AAOIFI Shariah Standard 57 — spot exchange, and a specific bar

AAOIFI, the standard-setter most Islamic financial institutions follow, issued a dedicated standard on gold in November 2016, developed with the World Gold Council. Two of its conditions decide the digital gold question.

First, when gold is bought with money, both sides must be delivered in the same session — physically or constructively. No deferred delivery. Second, constructive possession has a precise meaning: a specific bar, distinguishable from others by its serial number, must be allocated to the buyer, or the buyer must hold a certificate for such a bar issued on the day of the contract. The standard says plainly that unallocated gold may not be sold without physical possession.

What the standard says Clause 3/2/1: "In case of sale of gold for gold, or silver or currencies, the two counter-values must be delivered during the contracting session, physically or constructively." Clause 3/4: "Constructive possession is realized by allocation of the ingot and by enabling the buyer to dispose of it, or by holding a certificate that represents ownership of a specified ingot that is distinguishable (an allocated ingot) from others, by serial numbers or other distinct marks from other ingots, provided the certificate is issued the day the contract is concluded … it is not permissible to sell an unspecified ingot (technically known in the market as unallocated ingot) without physical possession." Clause 3/5/4: "Ingots stored in a warehouse and not distinguishable by serial numbers are considered unallocated and are thus subject to the Shari'ah rulings for joint ownership." — AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 57, Gold and Its Trading Controls, issued 19 November 2016. Islamonweb analysis — Indian platforms raise concerns

An analysis published on Islamonweb in March 2026 applied the AAOIFI standard to India's gold market specifically. On digital gold it found that customers buy fractional units that stay in the platform's custody, without segregated ownership records or a clear right to immediate delivery, and concluded that the platforms present Shariah concerns on possession. It reached a firmer conclusion on Sovereign Gold Bonds: that their fixed interest makes them non-compliant.

This is an analysis by a named author, not a fatwa from a darul ifta, and it is labelled as such here. Islamonweb is a different site from IslamWeb, which is quoted on the NPS page.

What the analysis says On digital gold: "investors purchase fractional gold units that remain under the platform's custody without a clear right to immediate delivery, contravening AAOIFI's requirement for qabḍ (possession)." On Sovereign Gold Bonds: they "involve fixed interest payments and cash settlement, rendering them non-compliant with Sharīʿah principles." — Ahammed Nijad PC, "Alignment of India's Gold Trading Frameworks with AAOIFI Sharīʿah Standards", Islamonweb, 26 March 2026, updated 6 April 2026. The possession test, point by point

This table sets the AAOIFI conditions next to what public information on Indian digital gold shows. It deliberately has no third column saying "passes" or "fails" — that judgement belongs to a scholar looking at a specific provider's terms, not to a comparison table.

AAOIFI Standard 57 conditions set against what public information shows about Indian digital gold
What AAOIFI Standard 57 requires What public information on Indian digital gold shows
Gold and money exchanged in the same session (3/2/1) Gold is credited to your account when you pay. Whether bars are bought and set aside that same day is not stated publicly.
A specific bar, identified by serial number, allocated to you (3/4) Your holding is a quantity in grams, often a fraction of a gram. It is not shown tied to any specific bar.
Or a certificate for a specified bar, issued the same day (3/4) No such certificate is described in the public information reviewed.
Unallocated gold not sold without physical possession (3/4) Physical delivery exists, but only later, as minted coins, above a minimum, and with charges.

The left column quotes the standard linked above. The right column describes public information and could be wrong about any single provider — a provider that does allocate specific, serial-numbered bars and issues a same-day certificate would be in a different position under this standard. That is exactly what to ask before buying.

Digital gold next to other ways to hold gold

Most people asking about digital gold are choosing between it and something else. The four common formats raise different Shariah questions, and one of them is not really a gold product at all.

Four ways to hold gold in India and the Shariah question each raises
Format What you actually own Regulated by Pays interest? The Shariah question
Physical gold The metal itself, in your hands Purity hallmarking by BIS No Spot exchange and possession at the counter
Digital gold A recorded quantity in shared, trustee-held vaults Neither SEBI nor RBI No Whether that recorded quantity counts as possession — see above
Gold ETF Units of a fund that holds physical gold SEBI No AAOIFI applies its gold rules to funds whose assets are entirely gold (10/3), so possession is again the question
Sovereign Gold Bond A loan to the Government of India, repaid at the gold price RBI, for the Government of India Yes — 2.5% a year on the amount invested Interest on a loan — a different question from possession entirely

Sovereign Gold Bonds: the Government has not issued new ones since February 2024 and said in the 2025 Budget that it had no immediate plans to, but existing bonds keep paying interest twice a year and trade on the NSE and BSE. Source: SGB scheme status, GoldenPi — as of 23 September 2026.

Why the SGB row matters

Sovereign Gold Bonds are often recommended as the "safest" way to hold gold, and for many Muslim investors that sounds like the obvious choice: government-backed, no storage risk, no GST. But an SGB is not gold you own. It is money lent to the government, repaid at whatever gold is worth at maturity, with interest paid on the way. The gold price is how the loan is measured, not what you hold.

What people do in practice

What follows describes approaches people take and the reasoning they give for them. It is not a list of options being recommended, and which one applies to you is a question for your scholar, not for this page.

Buy physical gold instead

Paying for coins or bars and taking them away in the same visit is the plainest way to meet the spot-and-possession condition, which is why some people who hold gold for Shariah reasons prefer it despite the storage and purity questions it brings.

Take delivery of what they hold

Converting a digital gold balance into minted coins ends the pooled-custody question for that portion. It costs minting and delivery charges, and needs the balance to reach the app's minimum, so it suits accumulated holdings more than small ones.

Ask the provider two questions first

Is my gold tied to a specific, serial-numbered bar? Is a certificate for it issued on the day I buy? Those are the two facts the AAOIFI test turns on, and they vary by provider — which is why the answer for one app may not hold for another.

Treat existing SGBs separately

Because the question there is interest rather than possession, it is a different conversation from the one about digital gold. Existing bonds pay into your bank account twice a year and can be sold on the exchange before maturity, which bears on what the options actually are.
