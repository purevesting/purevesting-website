Is Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana halal?

Scholars in India say no: it pays interest on money you choose to deposit, like PPF, so it is against Shariah. The catch: money already in an account keeps earning interest until it matures, 21 years after opening, and you can close it early only in a few cases.

Last updated 6 November 2026

How Sukanya Samriddhi works

Sukanya Samriddhi is a government savings scheme for a girl child, launched in 2015. A parent opens it in the daughter's name, deposits into it for 15 years, and the government adds interest at a rate it sets every quarter.

How the Sukanya Samriddhi scheme works
How it works Detail
Who opens it A parent or guardian, in the name of a daughter under 10
Accounts per family 2, with more allowed for twins or triplets
Rate 8.2% a year, compounded annually, set by the Ministry of Finance each quarter
Deposits ₹250 minimum and ₹1,50,000 maximum in a financial year, for 15 years
Maturity 21 years from opening — interest keeps being credited until then
Who runs the account The parent or guardian, until the girl turns 18
Taking money out early Up to 50% for higher education once she turns 18 or finishes Class 10; full closure early only in specific cases, such as her marriage after 18
Tax treatment Deduction under Section 80C, tax-free interest, tax-free maturity

Like PPF, it is a small savings scheme: deposits go into the National Small Savings Fund, which lends them on to the central and state governments at interest. The PPF page explains how.

What scholars say

Scholars have ruled on Sukanya Samriddhi by name twice: once a large group of Indian scholars, and once a Shariah board.

About 200 Indian scholars: against Shariah, because it pays interest

At a three-day meeting reported in March 2019, around 200 Islamic scholars passed a resolution saying the scheme is against Shariah. The reason was the interest itself: the balance grows each year by an interest credit.

What the resolution says "The bank annually increases the amount by [adding] annual interest up to nine percent. It is because that this scheme involves interest, it is clearly un-Islamic." — The resolution, as reported by PTI, 30 March 2019.

A Shariah board: not Shariah-compliant

A Shariah board that checks some of India's Shariah mutual funds answers the question on its own site: investing in Sukanya Samriddhi is not Shariah-compliant, because it is a loan that pays a fixed rate of interest.

What the board says Asked "Sukanya samridhi yojna is halal or haram and why?", the board answers that "investment in Sukanya Samridhi Yojana is Shariah non-compliant", describing it as a debt instrument with fixed interest. Its answer quotes an interest rate of 8.4%, from an earlier quarter — the rate has changed since, but the reasoning does not depend on it. — Frequently asked questions, 17 September 2022.

How this lines up with PPF

It works like PPF: you choose to deposit money you already own, and it is lent on at a fixed return. That is what the PPF fatwas treat as interest — unlike a compulsory provident fund, cut before your salary reaches you. No fatwa was found saying Sukanya Samriddhi is allowed. What happens if you stop depositing

This is what most parents asking need to know. Stopping deposits does not stop the interest.

What happens to a Sukanya Samriddhi account when deposits stop
If you… What happens
Miss the ₹250 minimum in a year The account goes into default. It can be revived until year 15 by paying the missed minimums plus a ₹50 penalty for each year missed. If it is never revived, the whole balance still earns the scheme's rate until the account is closed.
Keep depositing for the full 15 years Deposits stop after year 15, but interest keeps being credited until year 21.
Want to close it early Only allowed in specific cases, such as the girl's marriage after 18. There is no general option to close on request.
Want part of the money for education Up to 50% can be withdrawn once she turns 18 or finishes Class 10, against actual fees.

So if you opened an account years ago, the question is not only whether to keep depositing. It is also what to do with the interest that will keep coming, in your daughter's name, for years.

What people do

Not opening an account

Parents who follow the rulings above and haven't opened one save for the same goal another way, knowing that no Shariah-compliant option guarantees a return the way this scheme does.

Keeping the account, giving the interest away

Neither ruling above talks about an account you already have. Some parents keep it and give the interest to the poor each year — what a fatwa on PPF describes. That fatwa was about PPF, not Sukanya Samriddhi itself.

Paying only the minimum

Keeping the account alive at ₹250 a year avoids default penalties while adding as little new money as the rules allow. It does not reduce the interest already building up on the balance.

Asking with your daughter in mind

The account legally belongs to your daughter, not to you. People who ask a scholar usually say this first, because what can be done with a child's money is its own question.

Your passbook, from the post office or bank, shows the interest separately from your deposits.
