Is Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana halal?

Opening or paying in

Against Shariah

Scholars in India say so: it pays interest on money you choose to deposit, like PPF.

An account you already have

Interest keeps coming

Until it matures, 21 years after opening. You can close it early only in a few cases.

Moin Ali Syed
Last updated 8 November 2026

How Sukanya Samriddhi works

A government savings scheme for a girl child, launched in 2015.

  1. Your money

    A parent or guardian opens it in the name of a daughter under 10, and deposits ₹250 to ₹1,50,000 in a financial year, for 15 years.

  2. Where it goes

    Like PPF, into the National Small Savings Fund, which lends it on to the central and state governments at interest. The PPF page explains how.

  3. What you get

    8.2% a year, compounded annually, set by the Ministry of Finance each quarter. Interest is credited until the account matures, 21 years from opening.

    A Section 80C deduction, tax-free interest and tax-free maturity.

Read more: the scheme's other rules
  • Accounts per family: 2, with more allowed for twins or triplets.
  • Who runs the account: the parent or guardian, until the girl turns 18.
  • Taking money out early: up to 50% for higher education once she turns 18 or finishes Class 10; full closure early only in specific cases, such as her marriage after 18.

What scholars say

Scholars have ruled on Sukanya Samriddhi by name twice: once a large group of Indian scholars, and once a Shariah board.

About 200 Indian scholars, 2019

  • Against Shariah, because it pays interest

At a three-day meeting reported in March 2019, around 200 Islamic scholars passed a resolution. The reason was the interest itself: the balance grows each year by an interest credit.

What the resolution says "The bank annually increases the amount by [adding] annual interest up to nine percent. It is because that this scheme involves interest, it is clearly un-Islamic." — The resolution, as reported by PTI, 30 March 2019.

A Shariah board

  • Not Shariah-compliant: a loan that pays a fixed rate of interest

A Shariah board that checks some of India's Shariah mutual funds answers the question on its own site.

What the board says Asked "Sukanya samridhi yojna is halal or haram and why?", the board answers that "investment in Sukanya Samridhi Yojana is Shariah non-compliant", describing it as a debt instrument with fixed interest. Its answer quotes an interest rate of 8.4%, from an earlier quarter — the rate has changed since, but the reasoning does not depend on it. — Frequently asked questions, 17 September 2022.

It works like PPF: you choose to deposit money you already own, and it is lent on at a fixed return. That is what the PPF fatwas treat as interest — unlike a compulsory provident fund, cut before your salary reaches you. No fatwa was found saying Sukanya Samriddhi is allowed. If you stop depositing

Stopping deposits does not stop the interest. If you opened an account years ago, the question is also what to do with the interest that will keep coming, in your daughter's name, for years.

Read more: what happens in each case
What happens to a Sukanya Samriddhi account when deposits stop
If you… What happens
Miss the ₹250 minimum in a year The account goes into default. It can be revived until year 15 by paying the missed minimums plus a ₹50 penalty for each year missed. If it is never revived, the whole balance still earns the scheme's rate until the account is closed.
Keep depositing for the full 15 years Deposits stop after year 15, but interest keeps being credited until year 21.
Want to close it early Only allowed in specific cases, such as the girl's marriage after 18. There is no general option to close on request.
Want part of the money for education Up to 50% can be withdrawn once she turns 18 or finishes Class 10, against actual fees.

What people do

Not opening an account

Parents who follow the rulings save for the same goal another way, knowing that no Shariah-compliant option guarantees a return the way this scheme does.

Keeping it, giving the interest away

Some keep the account and give the interest to the poor each year — what a fatwa on PPF describes. Neither ruling above is about an account you already have.

Paying only the minimum

₹250 a year avoids default penalties with as little new money as the rules allow. It doesn't reduce the interest already building up.

Asking with your daughter in mind

The account legally belongs to your daughter, not to you, and what can be done with a child's money is its own question.

Your passbook, from the post office or bank, shows the interest separately from your deposits.
