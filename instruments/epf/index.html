Is EPF halal?

Scholars differ on the compulsory part: some fatwas say the extra amount is not riba (interest), so you may use it; others say it is riba, so give it away. On the voluntary top-up (VPF), most say it is riba, because you chose it.

Moin Ali Syed
Last updated 6 November 2026
On this page
  1. Where your EPF money goes
  2. What scholars say
  3. Why compulsory and voluntary are different
  4. What people do

Where your EPF money goes

EPFO invests your EPF money, and each year its Central Board of Trustees declares the interest rate. Where the money goes is public. EPFO follows an investment pattern set by the Department of Financial Services in the Ministry of Finance. It gives ranges, not fixed numbers, so the actual split moves within them.

EPFO's investment pattern for new money
Where it goes Share of new money What that is
Government securities 45–65% Loans to the central and state governments, paying interest
Other debt instruments 20–45% Bonds of public sector undertakings and highly rated companies, paying interest
Equity 5–15% Exchange-traded funds tracking broad market indices
Short-term debt Up to 5% Short-dated interest-bearing instruments

Most of the money is lent at interest. Government securities and company bonds are loans that pay a fixed return, which is riba in its plainest form. There is no grey area in these investments themselves.

The share part isn't Shariah-screened either. EPFO buys index funds (ETFs) that track the Nifty 50, the Sensex, the CPSE index and the Bharat 22 index. None of these use a Shariah screen, so that part includes banks, finance companies and other businesses a Shariah screen would remove.

What scholars say

A fatwa from India: not riba, you may take it

Asked about provident fund cut from salary by law, this fatwa calls the extra amount a gift from the employer or the government for your service, not interest. So you may take it and use it. It comes from India and is about Indian provident funds, so it fits EPF most closely.

What the fatwa says "The provident fund that the employees receive with double amount is a gift from the company or the government that is given to an employee after he is retired with good performance. It is not interest as per the Shariah." — Fatwa 1084/915/B=1432.

A fatwa from Sri Lanka: compulsory is salary, voluntary is not

This fatwa draws the line between compulsory and voluntary clearly. On the compulsory part, the amount credited is not riba but part of your salary, so you may take it. On voluntary contributions it says the opposite, and advises taking back only what you put in. It is about Sri Lanka's EPF and ETF, not India's.

What the fatwa says "The amount earned as interest on compulsory provident fund distribution does not fall in the [ambit] of riba (interest) but can be considered a part of employee's salary. It is therefore legitimate to receive this amount and use it for one's personal needs. However, if contribution to the provident fund is on a voluntary basis, then the amount earned as interest is [quite] similar to riba and is likely to become the means to earning interest. It is therefore recommended that the employee should forego amount of interest and accept the principal only."

A fatwa from the UK: VPF is not allowed

Asked about the Voluntary Provident Fund, this fatwa says VPF pays interest and joining it is your own choice, so it is not allowed and should be avoided. It is only about VPF, not compulsory EPF.

What the fatwa says "[A]s the Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF) scheme attracts interest in the payout to those who sign up for it, it will not be permissible … As it is a voluntary contribution it will need to be avoided." — Fatwa 05248, 17 April 2021.

A scholar's answer: if you have a choice, it is riba

If you have any say in whether interest is added to your fund, this scholar says it is riba and you should refuse it. If it is added anyway and you can't refuse it, work out the exact interest amount and give it to people in need instead of using it.

What the answer says "When you are given this discretion to allow your provident fund to be credited with the amount of interest, which these funds are getting on investment, that interest is indeed Riba. A God-fearing Muslim should refuse riba to be credited to him. If it gets added, nevertheless, by default … you should be aware of the exact amount of interest, so that you may draw that much, and pass it onto some poor or needy person(s), or institution(s) helping them out."

Why compulsory and voluntary are different

How can two scholars look at the same 8% credit and give opposite answers? It comes down to choice.

The fatwas that allow it don't say EPF's investments are halal. They say something narrower: when the law makes the deduction and you have no say, what comes back is better seen as pay you get later, from your employer or the state, than as a return you agreed to. You never signed up for interest, because you were never given the choice.

That reasoning stops working once you choose. With VPF, you put in extra money knowing it pays a fixed return. The thing that made the compulsory part acceptable — that you had no choice — is gone.

So "Is EPF halal?" can have two answers for one person. Someone who pays only the compulsory amount is in a different position from someone who has added VPF for years, even though both have one EPF account.

India's EPF has no Shariah option

Malaysia's EPF has a Shariah option next to its normal one, with its own Shariah committee and its own declared dividend. India's EPFO has nothing like it, so you can't ask for your balance to be invested the Shariah way.

What people do

Take it as pay

Following the fatwas from India and Sri Lanka, they treat the credit on the compulsory part as pay for their service and use it normally. On this view, nothing from that part needs to be given away.

Give the interest away

Following the view that the credit is riba, they find the interest in their yearly statement and give it away, without expecting reward, and keep what they put in. Several of the fatwas above say this is what to do when the credit can't be refused.

Stop or avoid VPF

Because the fatwas agree more closely against the voluntary top-up, some people pay only the compulsory amount into EPF and save the rest somewhere else. For them, the compulsory and voluntary parts are two different questions.

Ask before you touch the balance

EPF has withdrawal rules, tax effects and a linked pension, so leaving it is not only a Shariah question. The scholars above who talk about leaving advise asking first, not acting in a hurry.

Your yearly EPF statement shows the interest separately from what you paid in. You can get it from the EPFO member portal.
