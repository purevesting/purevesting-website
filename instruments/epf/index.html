Is EPF halal?

Compulsory EPF

Scholars differ

Some fatwas say the extra amount is not riba (interest), so you may use it. Others say it is riba, so give it away.

VPF, by choice

Most say riba

Because you chose it.

Moin Ali Syed
Last updated 8 November 2026

How EPF works

  1. Your money

    Paid in from your salary:

    • Your share Compulsory, by law
    • Your employer's Compulsory, by law
    • VPF, if you add it Your choice

  2. Where it goes

    EPFO invests it, and most of it is lent at interest. Share of new money:

    Government securities Loans to the central and state governments, paying interest 45–65%
    Other debt instruments Bonds of public sector undertakings and highly rated companies, paying interest 20–45%
    Equity Exchange-traded funds tracking broad market indices 5–15%
    Short-term debt Short-dated interest-bearing instruments Up to 5%

    0%50%100%

  3. What you get

    A yearly interest credit on your balance. EPFO's Central Board of Trustees declares the rate each year.

Read more: where the money goes, in detail

Where the money goes is public. EPFO follows an investment pattern set by the Department of Financial Services in the Ministry of Finance. It gives ranges, not fixed numbers, so the actual split moves within them.

Government securities and company bonds are loans that pay a fixed return, which is riba in its plainest form. There is no grey area in these investments themselves.

The share part isn't Shariah-screened either. EPFO buys index funds (ETFs) that track the Nifty 50, the Sensex, the CPSE index and the Bharat 22 index. None of these use a Shariah screen, so that part includes banks, finance companies and other businesses a Shariah screen would remove.

What scholars say

A fatwa from India

  • Compulsory: not riba; may be taken
  • VPF: not asked

Asked about provident fund cut from salary by law, it calls the extra amount a gift from the employer or the government for your service. It is about Indian provident funds, so it fits EPF most closely.

What the fatwa says "The provident fund that the employees receive with double amount is a gift from the company or the government that is given to an employee after he is retired with good performance. It is not interest as per the Shariah." — Fatwa 1084/915/B=1432.

A fatwa from Sri Lanka

  • Compulsory: not riba; part of salary
  • VPF: similar to riba; take back only what you put in

It draws the line between compulsory and voluntary clearly. It is about Sri Lanka's EPF and ETF, not India's.

What the fatwa says "The amount earned as interest on compulsory provident fund distribution does not fall in the [ambit] of riba (interest) but can be considered a part of employee's salary. It is therefore legitimate to receive this amount and use it for one's personal needs. However, if contribution to the provident fund is on a voluntary basis, then the amount earned as interest is [quite] similar to riba and is likely to become the means to earning interest. It is therefore recommended that the employee should forego amount of interest and accept the principal only."

A fatwa from the UK

  • Compulsory: not asked
  • VPF: not permissible

VPF pays interest and joining it is your own choice, so it should be avoided.

What the fatwa says "[A]s the Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF) scheme attracts interest in the payout to those who sign up for it, it will not be permissible … As it is a voluntary contribution it will need to be avoided." — Fatwa 05248, 17 April 2021.

A scholar's answer

  • Compulsory: riba where you have a choice; give it away
  • VPF: riba

If you have any say in whether interest is added, refuse it. If it is added anyway and you can't refuse it, work out the exact interest amount and give it to people in need.

What the answer says "When you are given this discretion to allow your provident fund to be credited with the amount of interest, which these funds are getting on investment, that interest is indeed Riba. A God-fearing Muslim should refuse riba to be credited to him. If it gets added, nevertheless, by default … you should be aware of the exact amount of interest, so that you may draw that much, and pass it onto some poor or needy person(s), or institution(s) helping them out."

India's EPF has no Shariah option. Malaysia's EPF has one next to its normal one, with its own Shariah committee and its own declared dividend. India's EPFO has nothing like it, so you can't ask for your balance to be invested the Shariah way.

Read more: why compulsory and voluntary get different answers

How can two scholars look at the same 8% credit and give opposite answers? It comes down to choice.

The fatwas that allow it don't say EPF's investments are halal. They say something narrower: when the law makes the deduction and you have no say, what comes back is better seen as pay you get later, from your employer or the state, than as a return you agreed to. You never signed up for interest, because you were never given the choice.

That reasoning stops working once you choose. With VPF, you put in extra money knowing it pays a fixed return. The thing that made the compulsory part acceptable — that you had no choice — is gone.

So "Is EPF halal?" can have two answers for one person. Someone who pays only the compulsory amount is in a different position from someone who has added VPF for years, even though both have one EPF account.

What people do

Take it as pay

Following the fatwas from India and Sri Lanka, they treat the credit on the compulsory part as pay for their service, and use it normally.

Give the interest away

Following the view that it is riba, they find the interest in their yearly statement, give it away without expecting reward, and keep what they put in.

Stop or avoid VPF

Because the fatwas agree more closely against the voluntary top-up, some pay only the compulsory amount and save the rest somewhere else.

Ask before you touch the balance

EPF has withdrawal rules, tax effects and a linked pension, so leaving it is not only a Shariah question. Ask first, and don't act in a hurry.

Your yearly EPF statement shows the interest separately from what you paid in. You can get it from the EPFO member portal.
