How much should you give away to purify your shares?

When a Shariah-compliant company earns some interest, its investors are expected to give away their share of that income to charity — this is called purification. There are three common ways to work out "your share", and they can differ a lot: one takes a slice of your dividend, while the other two take your share of all the interest the company earned that year. The calculator below works out all three for any company, so you can take the numbers to your scholar.

Work it out for your shares

Take a made-up company. This year it earned ₹10,000 crore in total, ₹120 crore of it as interest. It has 50 crore shares and paid a dividend of ₹6 a share. You held 100 of its shares for the whole year.

Put in any company's numbers and the table below updates.

The amount to give away under three purification methods
Method You would give
Dividend-based A slice of your dividend. Used by S&P's Shariah indices ₹7.20
AAOIFI method Your share of the year's interest, if you still held the shares at the company's year-end ₹240.00
Modified AAOIFI The same, scaled to the months you held them. Used by TASIS ₹240.00

This covers the interest the company earned. It does not work out anything on profit you make from selling shares.

With the starting numbers, the dividend-based method asks for ₹7.20 and the AAOIFI methods for ₹240 — over 30 times more. The gap comes from what each method starts from. The dividend-based method only purifies the part of the interest that reached you as a dividend. The AAOIFI methods count your share of all of it, paid out or not.

The three methods, and who uses them

The three formulas below are as TASIS sets them out. TASIS is the Shariah adviser that screens NSE's Shariah indices, the list behind this site's 205 figure.

1. Dividend-based

Interest income ÷ total income × dividend per share × shares you hold. S&P Dow Jones Indices publishes its version for its Shariah indices as the "dividend purification ratio". TASIS points out two weaknesses: it purges only a small part of the company's interest income, and in a year with no dividend, nothing is purged at all.

What the sources say "Dividends * (Non-Permissible Revenue / Total Income) … This ratio is provided to investors for purification purposes and is not a compliance ratio." — S&P Dow Jones Indices, S&P Shariah Indices methodology, September 2026 edition. Read the methodology

"It can be demonstrated that this method, in effect, leads to the purging of only a small part of the total interest income (from as little as under 5% to about 10% of the total interest income in the best case scenarios) … Also, in the event a company does not pay a dividend in a particular year, the interest income of that year is not purged at all." — TASIS, "Methodology of purging interest income". Read it

2. The AAOIFI method

Interest income ÷ the company's total shares × shares you hold at the company's year-end. This counts your share of all the interest the company earned that year, whether it paid a dividend or not. TASIS's objection: it puts the whole year's purification on whoever happens to hold the shares on the last day of the company's financial year.

3. Modified AAOIFI — the version TASIS uses

The AAOIFI amount × months you held the shares ÷ 12. It spreads the purification over the time you actually held the shares, so selling just before the year-end doesn't avoid it, and buying just before doesn't load you with the whole year. TASIS says it has adopted this version and calls it "logical and equitable".

What TASIS says On the AAOIFI method: "there appears to be a tenuous justification for laying the responsibility of purging the interest for the entire year solely and entirely on the person who is found to be holding the shares at the time of closing of the financial year."

On the modified method: "This method requires the purging of impure (interest) income earned per share of a company held by an investor pro-rated over the period for which the investor held those shares in his portfolio … The modified AAOIFI method of purification is logical and equitable." — TASIS, "Methodology of purging interest income". Read it

NSE's own methodology asks investors to purge "the pro rata portion of interest income accrued on their holding of shares", which reads closest to the second and third methods. Purevesting does not say which method is right. Which one to follow is a question for your scholar.

Where to find the numbers

  1. Total income and interest income — in the company's annual report, in the statement of profit and loss. "Total income" has a line of its own. Interest income sits inside "Other income": open the note that line points to. Take both figures from the same statement — reports usually have a standalone and a consolidated version.
  2. Total shares — the "Equity share capital" note in the same annual report gives the number of shares.
  3. Dividend per share — the dividend declared for that year, in the annual report or the company's announcement to the stock exchange.
  4. Your shares and months held — your broker's holdings statement.

Enter money in ₹ crore, as most Indian annual reports print it. If a report prints ₹ lakh or ₹ million instead, convert first: 1 crore is 100 lakh, or 10 million.
